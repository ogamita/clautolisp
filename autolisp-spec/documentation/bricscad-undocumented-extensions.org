* 1 Purpose and status

This page catalogues the Common-Lisp constructs that BricsCAD V26
silently accepts even though they appear in *neither* Autodesk's public
AutoLISP reference *nor* the BricsCAD-specific LISP documentation, and
records — per construct — what clautolisp does about each.

It is a *vendor-extensions catalogue*, not a normative part of the
AutoLISP / Visual LISP specification. Nothing here is promoted into
[[file:autolisp-visual-lisp-specification-draft.org][the spec draft]]; a
construct listed here is, by definition, an undocumented vendor
extension whose portability is a hazard, not a guarantee. The companion
tracking issue is ~issues/open/bricscad-undocumented-clisms.issue~.

* 2 Evidence base

** 2.1 The harvest probe

The probe
~autolisp-front-end/tests/scenarios/entities/bricscad-extensions-probe.lsp~
captures each construct's *raw return value* (each result wrapped in its
own ~princ~, so what is recorded is the form's own return value, not any
REPL/print-wrap echo). Every construct runs through
~vl-catch-all-apply~, so an unsupported construct records ~ERROR:<msg>~
instead of aborting the run. Output is the machine-readable
~EXTPROBE <KEY>=<VALUE>~ stream, terminated by ~EXTPROBE DONE~.

The probe is registered in the existing vendor-probes drivers
(~scripts/run-vendor-probes.{sh,ps1}~) as a HARVEST probe — it has no
per-vendor expectation file, so its transcript is saved as an artifact
and never gates the pipeline.

** 2.2 The confirming run

Source: ~vendor:probes:bricscad:macos~ (GitLab pipeline on ~master~ @
~b97d9fd~), run *2026-08-07* against real BricsCAD V26 on the macOS
runner. Artifact:
~dist/vendor-probes/bricscad/bricscad-extensions.log~.

* 3 Constructs

** 3.1 ~setf~

*** Vendor confirmation
2026-08-07 harvest, keys ~SETF-*~ and ~SETF-THEN-FOO~:

- ~SETF-NIL=2~, ~SETF-1=2~, ~SETF-2=2~, ~SETF-99=2~,
  ~SETF-STRING=2~, ~SETF-LIST=2~ — every ~(setf foo VALUE)~ returns the
  *constant* ~2~, whatever the value assigned (nil / 1 / 2 / 99 /
  ~"hello"~ / ~'(a b)~).
- ~SETF-THEN-FOO=nil~ — after ~(setf foo 42)~, reading ~foo~ back yields
  ~nil~: the earlier ~(setq foo nil)~ baseline is unchanged.

*** Observed semantics
BricsCAD V26's ~setf~ is *not* a ~setq~ alias. It does *not* assign to a
symbol place at all, and it returns a fixed internal sentinel ~2~
regardless of argument. It is a quirky non-assigning operation. (This
also resolves ~deferred-bricscad-setq-nil-returns-2.issue~: the "2" a
user once mistook for a ~setq~ bug came from a ~setf~ they typed
instead.)

*** clautolisp recommendation: *DON'T imitate*
clautolisp must NOT reproduce the "returns 2, assigns nothing" behaviour
— faking a broken vendor quirk helps no one. clautolisp therefore does
*not* define ~setf~. Under ~--dialect bricscad~ a ~(setf …)~ form is
left as an ordinary undefined function (an ~:undefined-function~ error),
rather than silently faking the sentinel. Should clautolisp ever want a
~setf~, it must be a clean clautolisp EXTENSION (CL-style generalised
assignment), never the BricsCAD "2" behaviour. When reached under a
portable-target dialect it is a candidate for an
~[ext-bricscad-undocumented]~ diagnostic (see §4; currently TODO, as the
construct is simply undefined).

** 3.2 ~let~

*** Vendor confirmation
2026-08-07 harvest, keys ~LET-SUM~ and ~LET-PARALLEL~:

- ~LET-SUM=3~ — ~(let ((x 1) (y 2)) (+ x y))~ ⇒ ~3~.
- ~LET-PARALLEL=(1 10)~ — with ~x~ set to 10 in the enclosing scope,
  ~(let ((x 1) (y x)) (list x y))~ ⇒ ~(1 10)~. The ~y~ init-form sees
  the *outer* ~x~ (= 10), NOT the let's ~x~ (= 1). Had the binding been
  sequential (a ~let*~), ~y~ would be 1 and the result ~(1 1)~.

*** Observed semantics
BricsCAD V26 provides a genuine Common-Lisp ~let~ with *parallel*
binding: all init-forms are evaluated in the enclosing scope, then the
variables are bound simultaneously; the body is an implicit progn
returning its last form's value.

*** clautolisp recommendation: *implement (parallel binding)*
This is the one genuine, cleanly-implementable BricsCAD extension.
clautolisp implements ~let~ as an AutoLISP special form with CL-style
parallel binding (~eval-let-form~ in
~clautolisp/autolisp-runtime/source/api.lisp~):

- Every init-form is evaluated against the *enclosing* scope first
  (parallel discipline, matching the ~(1 10)~ harvest).
- The variables are then bound simultaneously in a fresh dynamic frame,
  which shadows any surrounding binding for the extent of the body and
  is torn down on both normal and non-local exit — the same discipline
  as ~foreach~ and the usubr ~/~-locals frame.
- A binding is either a bare symbol (bound to ~nil~) or a
  ~(symbol init-form)~ pair; the body is an implicit progn (empty body
  ⇒ ~nil~).

Availability is dialect-gated. ~let~ is accepted under ~--dialect
bricscad~ / ~clautolisp~ / ~lax~. Under a portable-target dialect
(~--dialect strict~ / ~autocad~) it still evaluates but emits the
~[ext-bricscad-undocumented]~ warning (or errors when
~--portability-warning-mode error~) — see §4.

** 3.3 ~let*~

*** Vendor confirmation
2026-08-07 harvest, key ~LETSTAR-SEQ~:

- ~LETSTAR-SEQ=ERROR:no function definition <LET*>~ — evaluating
  ~(let* ((x 1) (y (+ x 1))) y)~ fails: BricsCAD V26 has no ~let*~.

*** Observed semantics
~let*~ *does not exist* on BricsCAD V26. This refutes the tracking
issue's original assumption that ~let*~ was a silent extension
alongside ~let~.

*** clautolisp recommendation: *not a vendor extension*
Do NOT add ~let*~ as a BricsCAD extension. clautolisp does *not* register
~let*~ as a special operator; a ~(let* …)~ form falls through to
ordinary function dispatch and is an ~:undefined-function~ error (never a
silently-accepted sequential-binding form). Should clautolisp ever want
~let*~, it would be a *pure clautolisp extension*, justified on its own
merits — not a claim of BricsCAD compatibility.

** 3.4 ~&rest~ (rest parameter)

*** Vendor confirmation
2026-08-07 harvest, key ~REST-BINDS~:

- ~REST-BINDS=(2 3 4)~ — ~((lambda (a &rest r) r) 1 2 3 4)~ ⇒ ~(2 3 4)~.

Bricsys support additionally confirmed ~&rest~ on 2026-06-07.

*** Observed semantics
BricsCAD V26 accepts the Common-Lisp ~&rest~ spelling (case-insensitive,
like every AutoLISP symbol) in ~defun~ / ~lambda~ lambda-lists: the
rest-parameter binds to the proper list of the surplus arguments.

*** clautolisp recommendation: *implemented*
Already implemented (clautolisp 1.1.16, ~variadic-functions.issue~).
clautolisp accepts both the ~&rest~ spelling and its own bare-~&~
spelling as synonyms. Dialect gating is via the existing
~[lambda-list-extension]~ warning (~emit-lambda-list-extension-warning~):
silent for ~&rest~ under ~--dialect bricscad~ / ~clautolisp~ / ~lax~;
warns under ~--dialect strict~ / ~autocad~.

** 3.5 bare ~&~ (rest separator)

*** Vendor confirmation
2026-08-07 harvest, key ~AMP-BINDS~:

- ~AMP-BINDS=ERROR:too few / too many~ — ~((lambda (a & r) r) 1 2 3 4)~
  is rejected: BricsCAD V26 does *not* treat a bare ~&~ as a
  rest-separator (it is read as an ordinary parameter name, so the arity
  no longer matches).

*** Observed semantics
The bare ~&~ separator is a clautolisp spelling only; BricsCAD V26 does
not accept it.

*** clautolisp recommendation: *reject under bricscad*
clautolisp keeps accepting bare ~&~ as a synonym of ~&rest~ under its own
~--dialect clautolisp~ (and ~lax~), but under ~--dialect bricscad~ the
bare ~&~ is flagged non-portable: the existing
~[lambda-list-extension]~ warning fires for ~&~ (but not ~&rest~) under
~--dialect bricscad~, matching the harvest. Under ~--dialect strict~ /
~autocad~ both spellings warn.

* 4 clautolisp dialect diagnostics

The ~[ext-bricscad-undocumented]~ diagnostic
(~emit-bricscad-undocumented-warning~ in
~clautolisp/autolisp-runtime/source/api.lisp~) reuses the existing
portability-warning infrastructure introduced for the lambda-list
extension (autolisp-spec ch.25):

- *Evaluation-tied*: it fires only when evaluation actually reaches the
  construct; a guarded (never-reached) occurrence is silent.
- *Once-per-source-occurrence*: deduped by the source object's identity
  (for ~let~, the binding-list cons), so a construct re-evaluated in a
  loop warns at most once per run.
- *Dialect matrix*:
  - ~--dialect bricscad~ — silent (native).
  - ~--dialect clautolisp~ — silent (blessed clautolisp extension).
  - ~--dialect lax~ — silent (catch-all "accept every vendor extension").
  - ~--dialect strict~, ~--dialect autocad~ — warn; portable AutoLISP has
    no such construct.
- *Escalation knob*: when the active dialect's ~portability-warning-mode~
  is ~:error~ (default ~:warn~), a non-silent occurrence signals a
  ~:non-portable-construct~ AutoLISP runtime error instead of printing.
- Warnings go to ~*error-output*~.

Currently the ~[ext-bricscad-undocumented]~ diagnostic is wired for
~let~. The bare-~&~ and ~&rest~ cases are covered by the pre-existing
~[lambda-list-extension]~ diagnostic. ~setf~ and ~let*~ are undefined
functions in clautolisp (no faking), so they surface as
~:undefined-function~ rather than a dedicated extension diagnostic — see
§5.

* 5 Open items / TODO

- *setf / let\* dedicated diagnostic*: ~setf~ and ~let*~ are intentionally
  left undefined in clautolisp, so a use of either raises
  ~:undefined-function~ today rather than a targeted
  ~[ext-bricscad-undocumented]~ note. Emitting the dedicated diagnostic
  for these would require recognising the names in the undefined-function
  resolution path (a small hook in ~resolve-unbound-function-or-signal~)
  without actually defining them. Deferred as low-value: the undefined
  error already prevents silent BricsCAD-only behaviour.
- *Bricsys support ticket*: a single ticket asking whether the ~setf~-"2"
  sentinel is intentional, and whether ~let~ is officially supported /
  deprecated / undocumented-but-stable, is still wanted.
